### Choice

Which idea you chose, why, and what you set aside.

**Chosen:** Room-based action platformer in the style of Castlevania titles (notably *Symphony of the Night* and *Aria of Sorrow*).

**Whip mechanic (core):** The player draws a shape in advance, then executes it in combat with a single button press. Shapes carry trade-offs, for example:
- Taller shapes are more defensive.
- Longer shapes with smaller hitboxes deal more damage.
- Damage scales down as whip length increases.

**Shape drawing:** Freeform, done in advance of combat (not in real time). The player keeps a loadout of 2-3 saved shapes at a time.
- Hard limit on whip length.
- Stat derivation (measuring the drawn shape to set defense, damage, hitbox, etc.) is a core part of the design.
- Smoothing/simplifying the drawn line is open to whatever solution best fits technical limits (to be decided during development).

**Scope decisions:**
- Whips are combat-only in the initial concept. Traversal and puzzle uses are stretch goals if time allows.
- The world is a small, explorable map of linked rooms. A final boss fight is a stretch goal.

**Why:**
- This is an idea I had a couple years ago but lacked the technical know-how to pull off, plus was busy with a half dozen other projects at the time, so it was put on the backburner. Now, with AI support, the idea is MUCH more feasible.
- The core idea is rooted heavily in my inspiration from the Castlevania anime miniseries, where the main character maneuvers his whip into whatever shape the moment requires, bending it in mid air, striking enemies with the tip, slashing it in front of himself to block attacks, etc.
- I've never found a game that features a system to recreate this, and I think it'll create opportunities for new and interesting gameplay.

**Set aside:** Arena survivor, boss-rush, and 1v1 duel as the main format; whip as traversal/puzzle tool (stretch goal only).

## Pitch

Keep this section to about 1,000 words.

### Concept

*Kinked Fury* is a 2D, room-based action platformer in the style of Castlevania's *Symphony of the Night* and *Aria of Sorrow*. Its central mechanic is an unrealistic whip that takes shapes impossible in real life: right angles, spirals, loops. Before combat, the player freely draws whip shapes and saves 2-3 to a loadout. In combat, one button press executes a shape. A shape's geometry sets its trade-offs: taller shapes defend better, longer shapes with smaller hitboxes hit harder, and damage falls as whip length grows. The player explores a small, linked map of rooms and fights enemies with these custom shapes.

### Title and branding

**Title:** Kinked Fury.

**Logo:** A coiled chain-whip line. The in-game whip is also a chain-whip rather than traditional leather, to fit the gothic tone.

**Visual branding:** Gothic pixel art in the vein of Castlevania, on a dark purple/black base, with electric cyan as the accent color. Aesthetics stay simple so development effort goes to the mechanics.

### Why this project

The idea is rooted in the whip combat of the Netflix *Castlevania* anime, where the protagonist bends his whip mid-air into whatever shape the moment requires: striking with the tip, or slashing it in front of himself to block. I have never found a game that recreates this, and I think it opens new and interesting gameplay. I first had this idea a few years ago but lacked the technical skill and time to build it. With AI support and my experience in Unity 2D, it is now feasible, and the five weeks are enough to test whether the core mechanic is fun.

### Users

Hobbyist gamers, particularly fans of Castlevania-style action platformers. They need tight, responsive mechanics and a whip system that is simple to learn but rewards experimentation. They do not need elaborate visuals or a large amount of content.

### Experience goals

Users should feel smart for finding a creative, effective whip shape, and excited to use it to defeat enemies. They should notice how a shape's geometry changes how it plays: height for defense, length and hitbox size for damage. Mastering shape design is a large part of the appeal, but the mechanic is tutorialized: the player starts with a loadout of basic shapes that demonstrate the pros and cons of different design approaches.

### Technical goals

The system must:
- Let the player freely draw a whip shape, with a hard limit on length.
- Derive stats (defense, damage, hitbox) from the drawn shape, smoothing or simplifying the line as needed for technical limits.
- Save a loadout of 2-3 shapes and execute one per button press, with reliable collision against enemies and walls.
- Provide starting shapes that teach the trade-offs.

Technical challenges to take on: the drawing-to-whip pipeline (turning an arbitrary line into animated whip behavior, stats, and a hitbox), enemy AI, room transitions across a linked map, and loadout save data.

### Starting point

From scratch. No previous or open-source code is reused.

### Tech stack

- **Unity 6 / C# (2D):** I have about five years of continuous Unity use across a couple dozen projects, mostly 2D.
- **AI image generation for most art assets:** I have limited illustration skills, so generated images are the main art source. The specific tool will be decided with the Studio director, and its license must permit public release.
- **Photoshop and Aseprite:** to clean up AI-generated images and finish pixel art as needed.
- **Desktop Windows build:** chosen over WebGL because of past problems with WebGL resolution and screen resizing.

### MVP or critical function

The player draws a freeform whip shape, saves it to the loadout, and uses it in a room to defeat 2-3 different enemy types: a melee enemy, a flying enemy, and a long-range projectile enemy.

The riskiest function is the drawing-to-whip pipeline, so it is validated first. It works when a drawn shape becomes a whip that matches the drawing, derives stats that follow the stated trade-offs, and hits enemies reliably, and when each enemy type can be defeated with at least two meaningfully different shapes.

### Five-week map

Art is developed alongside the mechanics in every phase (player, whip, and enemy animations), because changing art later can change how mechanics behave.
1. **Weeks 1-2: Whip prototype and refinement.** Freeform drawing, length limit, shape playback, stat derivation, single-button attack, and tuning against extreme drawings. Placeholder art, early animations, and first AI art consistency tests.
2. **Week 3: Combat and enemies.** Player movement, shape loadout, starting shapes, and the three enemy types with animations and AI.
3. **Week 4: Map and rooms.** Linked map, room transitions, loadout save data, gothic tilesets and backgrounds, and tutorialization of shape trade-offs.
4. **Week 5: Polish and release.** Balance, bug fixing, playtesting, final art cleanup, and a public Windows build.

### Stretch goals

1. Boss fight.
2. Larger map.
3. Traversal mechanics (whip used for movement).
4. Puzzles (whip used to solve spatial problems).

### Risks

- **AI art consistency.** I have little experience keeping AI-generated characters, enemies, and animation frames visually consistent. Early test: generate and clean up the player and one enemy in Week 1, and check that they match in style and animate cleanly. If they don't, I simplify the art style or increase cleanup in Aseprite.
- **Drawing-to-whip pipeline.** Freeform lines may produce unreliable collision, unbalanced stats, or poor feel. Early test: the Week 1 prototype, tested with deliberately extreme drawings.
- **Scope.** Five weeks is tight for combat, three enemy types, a linked map, and art. Early check: review progress against the phase map at the end of each week and cut from the stretch goals first.
